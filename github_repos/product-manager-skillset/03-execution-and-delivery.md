# 3. Execution & Delivery

Execution is the craft of **turning decisions into shipped software** on time, at quality, with the intended outcome. It is the day-to-day heartbeat of the PM role.

## Sub-skills

- **Writing PRDs / specs** — clear problem, goals, non-goals, requirements, edge cases, success metrics.
- **User story authoring** — INVEST criteria, acceptance criteria, Gherkin (Given/When/Then).
- **Agile & Scrum fluency** — sprint planning, standups, retros, backlog grooming, story points.
- **Alternative processes** — Kanban, Shape Up (Basecamp), dual-track agile.
- **Working with engineering** — scoping, MVP negotiation, tech-debt trade-offs, unblocking.
- **Working with design** — design reviews, feedback quality, respecting design authority.
- **Launch management** — beta programs, phased rollouts, feature flags, kill switches, comms plans.
- **QA & release readiness** — acceptance testing, launch checklists, post-launch monitoring.
- **Incident & bug triage** — severity assessment, prioritization vs. new work.

## Why it matters

Strategy without execution is a slide deck. PMs who ship consistently earn the credibility to make bigger strategic bets later.

## What great looks like

- Team knows exactly what to build and why before sprint starts.
- PRDs anticipate edge cases; engineers rarely ask "what about X?".
- Ships predictably; slippage is communicated early, not on launch day.
- Post-launch, tracks metrics and drives iteration rather than moving on.

## Common pitfalls

- Writing solutions before problems.
- Micro-managing engineers on *how* instead of clarifying *what/why*.
- Treating launch as the finish line instead of the starting line.
- Skipping retros or treating them as blame sessions.
