# 8. Design & UX Sense

PMs are not designers, but they are **the second-most-responsible person for user experience quality** on the team. Strong UX sense means you spot problems early and give useful feedback.

## Sub-skills

- **Design principles** — hierarchy, contrast, alignment, consistency, feedback, affordance.
- **Usability heuristics** — Nielsen's 10 heuristics as a critique lens.
- **Interaction patterns** — knowing standard patterns so you don't reinvent (and confuse users).
- **Information architecture** — navigation, taxonomy, mental models.
- **Accessibility (a11y)** — WCAG basics, keyboard nav, screen readers, color contrast, inclusive language.
- **Content design / UX writing** — button labels, empty states, error messages, tone of voice.
- **Prototyping literacy** — reading Figma files, understanding component/variant systems.
- **Design critique** — giving specific, non-prescriptive feedback ("users may struggle to find X" vs. "make it blue").
- **User testing** — running quick 5-user usability tests.
- **Design systems** — why they exist, when to extend vs. create new components.

## Why it matters

Users don't distinguish between "product" and "design"; they experience one thing. PMs with UX sense ship experiences that feel cohesive and intuitive.

## What great looks like

- Respects the designer's authority but pushes on user outcomes.
- Gives feedback on *problems*, not *solutions*.
- Catches accessibility and edge-case states before launch.
- Can sketch a rough flow to communicate an idea (fidelity is irrelevant).

## Common pitfalls

- Redlining designs like a junior designer.
- Prioritizing new features over fixing broken flows.
- Treating accessibility as a "later" feature.
- Confusing "I like it" with "users will succeed with it".
