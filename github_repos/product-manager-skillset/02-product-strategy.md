# 2. Product Strategy

Strategy is the set of **choices about where to play and how to win**. A PM sets strategy at the feature, product, or portfolio level depending on seniority.

## Sub-skills

- **Vision setting** — a compelling 3–5 year picture of the product's future state.
- **Positioning & differentiation** — who the product is for, who it is *not* for, and why to pick it over alternatives.
- **Roadmapping** — sequencing bets over quarters/years; now-next-later, outcome-based roadmaps.
- **Prioritization frameworks** — RICE, ICE, MoSCoW, Kano, Weighted Shortest Job First (WSJF), cost of delay.
- **OKRs & goal setting** — writing outcome (not output) objectives with measurable key results.
- **Business model design** — freemium vs. subscription vs. transactional vs. marketplace vs. enterprise.
- **Competitive strategy** — Porter's Five Forces, SWOT, competitive teardowns, moats.
- **Trade-off reasoning** — saying "no" with a clear rationale grounded in strategy.

## Why it matters

Without strategy, teams ship features that don't compound. Strategy turns scattered work into a coherent story that customers, execs, and investors can rally around.

## What great looks like

- Can explain the product strategy in 60 seconds to any audience.
- Every roadmap item traces back to a strategic pillar or outcome.
- Kills popular ideas that don't fit the strategy.
- Balances short-term revenue with long-term positioning.

## Common pitfalls

- Confusing a roadmap with a strategy.
- Vision statements that are generic ("delight users").
- Chasing competitors instead of customer outcomes.
- Prioritization by loudest-voice-in-the-room.
