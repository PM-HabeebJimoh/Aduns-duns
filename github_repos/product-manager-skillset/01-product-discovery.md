# 1. Product Discovery

Discovery is the practice of figuring out **what is worth building** before committing engineering effort. A PM who is strong at discovery reduces the risk of shipping features nobody wants.

## Sub-skills

- **User research** — planning and running interviews, surveys, diary studies, usability tests.
- **Problem framing** — turning fuzzy complaints into a crisp problem statement (Jobs-to-be-Done, "How Might We").
- **Opportunity sizing** — estimating TAM/SAM/SOM, addressable users, revenue upside.
- **Hypothesis-driven thinking** — writing testable assumptions and designing the cheapest experiment to falsify them.
- **Continuous discovery** — maintaining a weekly cadence of customer contact (Teresa Torres' opportunity solution trees).
- **Synthesis** — turning raw notes into insights, themes, and prioritized opportunities.
- **Concept validation** — prototypes, wireframes, concierge tests, Wizard-of-Oz, smoke tests, fake-door tests.

## Why it matters

Roughly two-thirds of shipped features fail to move the metrics they were built for. Discovery is the leverage point: an hour spent invalidating a bad idea saves months of engineering.

## What great looks like

- Talks to ≥ 3 users every week without being told to.
- Can articulate the top 5 unmet needs of the target segment from memory.
- Kills their own ideas quickly when evidence disagrees.
- Distinguishes between what users **say** and what they **do**.

## Common pitfalls

- Confirmation bias in interviews (leading questions).
- Confusing feature requests for underlying needs.
- Skipping discovery under delivery pressure.
- Talking only to internal stakeholders instead of real users.
