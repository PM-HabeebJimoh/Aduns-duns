# 6. Technical Fluency

You don't need to code production systems, but you must **understand the material your team works with** well enough to make trade-offs and earn engineering trust.

## Sub-skills

- **Systems thinking** — how frontend, backend, database, and third-party services fit together.
- **APIs** — REST vs. GraphQL vs. gRPC, request/response, auth (OAuth, JWT), rate limits, webhooks.
- **Data models** — entities, relationships, primary/foreign keys, why schema decisions are hard to reverse.
- **Databases** — SQL vs. NoSQL, indexes, transactions, eventual consistency.
- **Web & mobile basics** — client-server, browsers, cookies, iOS/Android release cycles, app store review.
- **Infrastructure basics** — cloud providers, CDN, caching, latency, uptime/SLO/SLA.
- **Security & privacy** — authn vs. authz, PII, GDPR/CCPA, threat modeling at a high level.
- **AI/ML literacy** — supervised vs. unsupervised, embeddings, LLMs, prompts, RAG, evals, hallucinations, cost/latency trade-offs.
- **Reading code (lightly)** — able to skim a PR diff and follow the logic.
- **Estimation intuition** — knowing when "1 week" vs. "1 quarter" is realistic.

## Why it matters

Engineers respect PMs who understand the trade-offs they're being asked to make. Technical fluency also unlocks better scoping and unblocks decisions faster.

## What great looks like

- Can whiteboard the system architecture of their own product.
- Anticipates technical objections in PRDs before eng raises them.
- Knows when to push back on "it's not possible" and when to accept it.
- Reads the ML/AI literature well enough to spot vendor hype.

## Common pitfalls

- Pretending to understand — engineers see through it instantly.
- Treating tech debt as always deferrable.
- Ignoring non-functional requirements (performance, security, accessibility).
